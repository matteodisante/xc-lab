\section{Data}

The archive of the French free-flight federation (FFVL) holds \LtFlightsAll{} paraglider flights
recorded between \LtFirstYear{} and \LtLastYear. We use the \LtFlights{} flights that depart and
are logged at most every \LtLoggerMax~s. The pilot declares the type of each flight. Of these flights
\FlightsOpen{} are declared open (a free distance or a route with up to three turn points),
\FlightsClosed{} closed (a triangle, a quadrilateral or an out-and-return) and \FlightsNeither{}
neither. \pending{State how the tracks are cleaned (spikes, gaps, altitude); the rule is in the
preprocessing of xc-dynamics and is not reported in the xc-lab sections read for this text.}

The opening of a flight is the distance from its takeoff to its landing over the path flown. It is 1
for a straight line and close to 0 for a flight that returns to its takeoff. Declared open flights
are often folded: their median opening is \MedianEvery{}, and \BelowEvery\% of them have an opening
below \MinOpening. A flight that returns near its takeoff moves away and then back, so its
displacement stops growing as the lag approaches its duration and its mean squared displacement (MSD)
bends. Closed circuits therefore bend the MSD at long lags. On the dataset to \DeclTauLongest~s
(section Persistence) the local exponent at that lag falls to \DeclLongestHS{} for solo flights (S) and \DeclLongestHA{} for the same flights alone
(A), and group time (G) keeps \DeclLongestHG{} on \DeclLongestFlightsG{} flights only. The flights
of G are the most open at these lags (Table~\ref{tab:opening}), so part of the straighter progress of
G at long lags is the shape of its flights.

We keep the declared open flights with an opening above \MinOpening, \Kept{} flights or \KeptShare\%
of the declared open ones, and all comparisons of the main text use them. We chose \MinOpening{} as
the lowest threshold that removes the flights that return near their takeoff and that keeps similar
shares of the flights from low and high takeoffs, \TakeoffLowKeep\% below and \TakeoffHighKeep\%
above \MountainM~m. A stricter threshold of \Stricter{} keeps only \TakeoffLowStrict\% and
\TakeoffHighStrict\% of them. It would select the region as well as the shape and raise the
exponent by construction.

\section{Phases}

Every flight logged at 1~Hz is cut into climbs, searches and transitions by the labelling of
Ref.~\cite{Vilpellet2026}. A climb is a stretch of circling in which the glider gains height, a
transition a straight glide between two climbs, and a search a stretch flown with turns and without a
sustained gain. Phases exist on the flights logged at 1~Hz, so the phase statistics rest on fewer
flights than the MSD: in the raw comparison \RawPhaseFlightsS{} flights of S, \RawPhaseFlightsA{} of A
and \RawPhaseFlightsG{} of G, against \RawFlightsOneS, \RawFlightsOneA{} and \RawFlightsOneG{} for the
MSD.

We use four statistics: the time in climb, the share of searches that end in a climb (a search ends
in a climb when the next phase of the flight is a climb), the glide speed (the straight-line distance
of the transitions over their duration, in km/h) and the height lost per km of glide (m/km). A phase
belongs to the unit that holds its middle. The unit is the flight in the raw design, the piece or the
control segment in the same-route design and the window in the same-flight design. For a unit $u$ let
$n_u$ and $d_u$ be the numerator and the denominator of the statistic and $T_u$ its phase time. The
statistic of a population is the ratio of averages
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sum_u w_u\, n_u/T_u}{\sum_u w_u\, d_u/T_u},
\end{equation}
with $w_u$ the weight of the unit (one, or the bootstrap count in a draw). For the time in climb
$n_u$ is the time in climbs and $d_u = T_u$. We average before we divide so that a unit with a single
search does not give 0\% or 100\%, and no long flight dominates.

\section{Groups}

Appendix~A of the main text gives the identification rule. Table~\ref{tab:groups} lists its
parameters. Contacts are sought among all flights of the archive, so a pilot may meet any other pilot
whose track was uploaded. Only uploaded tracks are seen, and a group may have more members than we
count. With these values \LtGroupFlights{} flights (\LtGroupShare\% of the eligible flights) fly in a
group at some point, for \LtGroupHours{} flight-hours, and groups of two pilots hold \LtPairShare\% of
this time.

A flight is split into stretches on its own clock. After its departure, every instant of a flight
that entered a group at least once is in a group or alone. We compare three populations: G, the
stretches in a group of the flights that entered one; A, the other stretches of those flights, flown
by the same pilots on the same gliders and days; and S, the flights that never entered a group. A
piece is a stretch within one segment of the track, on a \TnGridStep~s grid of the track clock.

\section{Persistence}\label{sec:persistence}

For a flight $f$ and a population, the time-averaged MSD pools the origins of all its pieces in that
population,
\begin{equation}
\overline{\delta^2_f}(\tau) = \frac{1}{N_f(\tau)} \sum_t \bigl\| \mathbf{r}(t+\tau) - \mathbf{r}(t) \bigr\|^2 ,
\end{equation}
with $t$ and $t+\tau$ in the same piece, on a \TnGridStep~s grid. The ensemble $\mathrm{MSD}(\tau)$ is
the mean over the flights, with one weight per flight (per piece, control segment or window in the
other designs). The local exponent $\Hloc(\tau)$ is half the slope of $\ln \mathrm{MSD}$ against
$\ln\tau$ over the lags within $\pm\LtDecade$ decades of $\tau$.

A lag $\tau$ needs pieces of at least $\tau/\LtSupport$~s, so that the origins of every piece cover the
lag in the same share of it. A dataset to the lag $T$ keeps in each population the pieces of at least
$T/\LtSupport$~s and measures them at every lag up to $T$, so that the population does not change along a
curve. We use two datasets. Dataset \LtTauOne{} has $T = \RawTauOne$~s and pieces of at least
\RawShortestOne~s. Dataset \LtTauTwo{} has $T = \RawTauTwo$~s and pieces of at least \RawShortestTwo~s. The
value $\Hloc(\LtLagOne~\mathrm{s})$ is read on dataset \LtTauOne{}, where its window is cut at \LtLagOne~s.
$\Hloc(\LtLagTwo~\mathrm{s})$ is read on dataset \LtTauTwo{}, where its window of $\pm\LtDecade$ decades would run from
\HqLowest{} to \HqHighest~s and is cut at \RawTauTwo~s. We chose \LtTauTwo~s as the longest lag at which the
same-route design keeps \MatMin{} pieces of G per level of the wing. Table~\ref{tab:raw} gives the
raw design.

\section{Uncertainty}

Appendix~B of the main text describes the two-way bootstrap of pilots and blocks~\cite{Cameron2011}.
We use \NBoot{} draws of pilots, of blocks and of cells, and the same draws for all the quantities of
a design, so that a difference of two quantities has the band of its own draws. We checked the
coverage on synthetic data that keep the real flights, pilots, blocks, pieces and events of the three
designs and carry no group effect, in \CovReplicates{} replicates of several mixes of the variances of
pilots, blocks, flights and units, with \CovDraws{} draws per bootstrap. A 90\% band holds the true
value in \CovNewMin--\CovNewMax\% of the replicates, and its standard error is \CovNewSdMin{} to \CovNewSdMax{}
times the standard deviation of the estimate over the replicates. A bootstrap over blocks of episodes alone, without
pilots, holds it in only \CovOldRawMin\% of the replicates when pilots matter. A band that excludes 0
is a two-sided test at 10\%, so among many quantities without an effect about one in ten excludes 0
by chance. The bands do not include the choice of the matching rules, which the section Robustness
varies, nor the choice of the threshold of the Hill estimator.

\section{Same-route design}

\emph{Matching.} For each piece of G, the controls are stretches of flights of S on another date. A
control matches the piece when a share \MatCover{} of its points, taken every \LtMatchStep~s, lies within
\MatRho~m of the control track and the control flew them in the same direction. The control stretch
runs between the points of its track nearest to the first and to the last of these points, and
lasts at least as long as the dataset needs. Each piece may have several controls, which share its
weight. A caliper then keeps the controls of the same season (month within \MatMonth), the same hour
(within \MatHour{} hours, local time of the middle of the piece and of the stretch) and a wing of the
same class. The pool holds \MatPool{} flights of S and gives \MatMatches{} matches before the
caliper. We chose the caliper by trying the narrower ones first and taking the first that keeps at
least \MatMin{} pieces of G per level of the wing on dataset \LtTauTwo{}: month within \MatMonth, hour within
\MatHour, same class. It keeps \MatNGPieces{} pieces of G from \MatNGFlightsGSide{} flights and
\MatNGPilotsGSide{} pilots on dataset \LtTauOne{}, with \MatNGControls{} control stretches, and
\MatNAPieces{} pieces of A from \MatNAFlightsASide{} flights and \MatNAPilotsASide{} pilots, with
\MatNAControls{} control stretches. We write S$|$G and S$|$A for the controls of the pieces of G and of
A.

\section{Same-flight design}

\emph{Windows.} For each stretch of a flight in a group we cut four windows of \PreWindow~s on the
clock of the flight. Pre is the window before the stretch, if the flight was alone for at least that
long. Start and end are its first and last \PreWindow~s, if it lasts that long. Post is the window
after it, if the flight is alone for that long. A set of four windows is one event. The MSD is measured
on \PreLags{} lags of the grid, up to \PreMaxLag~s, the largest within $\LtSupport\times\PreWindow$~s. The
changes at entry and at exit are $D_\mathrm{in} = \Hloc^\mathrm{start} - \Hloc^\mathrm{pre}$ and
$D_\mathrm{out} = \Hloc^\mathrm{end} - \Hloc^\mathrm{post}$, and
\begin{equation}
g = \frac{D_\mathrm{in} + D_\mathrm{out}}{2}, \qquad d_w = \frac{D_\mathrm{in} - D_\mathrm{out}}{2}
\end{equation}
are the effect of the group and the drift over one window. If the quantity drifts linearly with the
flight clock, $D_\mathrm{in}$ is the effect plus the drift and $D_\mathrm{out}$ the effect minus it,
so that $g$ is free of the drift. A profile with curvature along the flight is not removed. If
persistence were low after takeoff and before landing and high between, $g$ would be positive with no
effect of the group. We test this with two controls.

\emph{Controls.} The control pairs are two consecutive windows of \PreWindow~s drawn at random in solo
time, in the same flights alone (\PreEventsMSDCA{} events) and in flights of S (\PreEventsMSDCS{}
events); the change is the later window minus the earlier. Both stay close to 0
(Table~\ref{tab:prepost}), so a linear drift is small. The mock events place the four windows of a
real event at the same times of the flight clock in flights of S. For each of the real events we draw
at random up to \PreMockK{} flights of S in which the four windows fit in one solo stretch at these
times, within the caliper of the same-route design (\PreMockCaliper). We measure and compare them like the real windows. A mock event takes the
resampling cell of its real event, and the mocks of one event weigh one event. This gives
\PreMockEvents{} mock events in \PreMockFlights{} flights, for \PreMockRealEvents{} of the
\PreEvents{} real events. In a second variant the mock flight also took off at the site of the real
one: \PreMockSiteEvents{} mock events in \PreMockSiteFlights{} flights, for \PreMockSiteRealEvents{}
real events. Since the real events with a mock are a subset, we report $g$ on that subset too and
the difference of the two.

\emph{Length of the window.} $g$ at \LtLagOne~s is \PreWinBHK{} \PreWinBHKBand{} with windows of
\PreWinBLen~s and \PreWinDHK{} \PreWinDHKBand{} with windows of \PreWinDLen~s
(Table~\ref{tab:prepost}). With windows of \PreWinALen~s it falls to \PreWinAHK{} \PreWinAHKBand. At
\LtLagOne~s the window is \LtWinShortShare{} of its length, close to the limit of the $\tau/\LtSupport$ rule, and
$D_\mathrm{in}$ is \PreWinADIn{} while $D_\mathrm{out}$ is \PreWinADOut, so the exponent at \LtLagOne~s is
not comparable there. At the lag \LtLagTwo~s, which the windows of \PreWinCLen{} and \PreWinDLen~s allow,
$g$ is \PreWinCHQ{} \PreWinCHQBand{} on the first and \PreWinDHQ{} \PreWinDHQBand{} on the second. The
two bands overlap and the events differ (\PreWinCEvents{} and \PreWinDEvents), so we do not say that
the two differ.

\section{Cross-country speed and phases}

\emph{Cross-country speed.} In a piece, a control stretch or a window, the speed is the mean over the
origins $t$ on the grid of the straight-line distance $\|\mathbf{r}(t+\LtXcMin~\mathrm{min}) -
\mathbf{r}(t)\|$ divided by \LtXcMin~min, in km/h. It needs pieces of at least $\LtXcS/\LtSupport$~s. The raw design
uses \RawXcFlightsS{} flights of S, \RawXcFlightsA{} of A and \RawXcFlightsG{} of G. Raw, the speed is
\RawStatXcS{} \RawStatXcSBand{} km/h for S, \RawStatXcA{} \RawStatXcABand{} for A and \RawStatXcG{}
\RawStatXcGBand{} for G, so that G $-$ S is \RawGapXc{} \RawGapXcBand, A $-$ S \RawGapXcAS{}
\RawGapXcASBand{} and G $-$ A \RawGapXcGA{} \RawGapXcGABand~km/h. On the same route, on
\MatXcPiecesG{} pieces of G and \MatXcPiecesA{} of A, G $-$ S$|$G is \MatDeltaXcG{} \MatDeltaXcGBand{}
and A $-$ S$|$A \MatDeltaXcA{} \MatDeltaXcABand: the same flights alone go as fast as their controls,
and the effect of the group at fixed route and pilot is \MatDeltaXcD{} \MatDeltaXcDBand~km/h. In the
same flight $g$ is \PreGXc{} \PreGXcBand{} and $d_w$ is \PreDwXc{} \PreDwXcBand. The mock events give
\PreMockGXc{} \PreMockGXcBand, and $g$ minus mock is \PreNetGXc{} \PreNetGXcBand~km/h
(Table~\ref{tab:speed}).

\emph{Phases.} Raw, a search ends in a climb \RawStatFoundG\% of the time in G against
\RawStatFoundS\% in S. On the same route, net of selection, the time in climb grows and the glides do
not change (Table~\ref{tab:speed}). The glide statistics have different signs in the same-route and in
the same-flight designs. On the same route G glides slower and steeper than S$|$G
(\MatDeltaGlideG{} \MatDeltaGlideGBand~km/h and \MatDeltaSinkG{} \MatDeltaSinkGBand~m/km), and A does
too (\MatDeltaGlideA{} \MatDeltaGlideABand~km/h and \MatDeltaSinkA{} \MatDeltaSinkABand~m/km), so that
the effect at fixed route and pilot is \MatDeltaGlideD{} \MatDeltaGlideDBand~km/h and
\MatDeltaSinkD{} \MatDeltaSinkDBand~m/km. Within a flight a pilot in a group glides faster and
flatter than before and after.

\section{Tail of transitions}

\emph{Threshold.} The Hill estimate depends on the threshold $x_\mathrm{min}$. Following
Ref.~\cite{Clauset2009} we take the $x_\mathrm{min}$ that minimises the Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance
between the data above it and the fitted law,
\begin{equation}
D(x_\mathrm{min}) = \max_{x \ge x_\mathrm{min}} \Bigl| F_\mathrm{data}(x) - \bigl[1 - (x/x_\mathrm{min})^{-\hat\mu}\bigr] \Bigr| .
\end{equation}
We choose it on the solo flights of the raw design, \TlXmin~s, with \TlRawMuSN{} of their \TlRunsS{}
transitions above it, and read every population and class of the three designs at this one value so
that the tails are compared on the same range. A scan of $D$ over \LtKsN{} thresholds between \LtKsLow{} and
\LtKsHigh~s puts its minimum at \TlKsXminS~s for S, \TlKsXminA~s for A and \TlKsXminG~s for G. The bands are
those of the two-way bootstrap at the threshold held fixed. They do not include the uncertainty of the
threshold, which the Hill plot shows (Fig.~\ref{fig:tail}b). On the same route a transition of a piece
weighs 1 and a transition of a control $1/m$, with $m$ the number of controls of its piece.

\emph{Raw.} The index rises with the threshold in the three populations, and the gap G $-$ S opens with
it: \TlHillGapSixHundred{} at \TlHillGapSixHundredXmin~s, \TlHillGapThousand{} at
\TlHillGapThousandXmin~s, \TlHillGapThreshold{} at \TlHillGapThresholdXmin~s and \TlHillGapTwoThousand{}
at \TlHillGapTwoThousandXmin~s. The bands of G and S stay apart for every threshold between
\LtTailApartFrom{} and \LtTailApartTo~s. The size of the gap depends on where the threshold is put.
The climbs and the searches show no difference between the populations.

\emph{Same flight.} In a window of \PreWindow~s a transition cannot be longer than the threshold, so
we class instead every transition of the \TlFlightFlights{} flights with a group stretch by the
stretch that holds it. A transition is before if it lies whole in the solo time before the first group
stretch, in group if it lies whole in a group stretch, after if it lies whole in the solo time after the
last one, between if it lies whole in the solo time between two stretches, and spanning if it ends
after the stretch where it starts. Above the threshold there are \TlFlightMuBeforeN{} transitions
before, \TlFlightMuInN{} in group, \TlFlightMuAfterN{} after, \TlFlightMuBetweenN{} between and
\TlFlightMuSpanningN{} spanning. In analogy with $g$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_\mathrm{in} = \hat\mu_\mathrm{in} - \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\hat\mu_\mathrm{before} + \hat\mu_\mathrm{after}\bigr),
\qquad
\Delta_{AB} = \hat\mu_\mathrm{after} - \hat\mu_\mathrm{before} .
\end{equation}
We expected before and after to be alike and the group smaller. We find $\Delta_\mathrm{in}$ of
\TlFlightDeltaIn{} \TlFlightDeltaInBand{} and $\Delta_{AB}$ of \TlFlightDeltaAfterBefore{}
\TlFlightDeltaAfterBeforeBand. After a group the long transitions include the final glides to landing:
\TlDescFlightLastAfter\% of them are the last phase of the flight, against \TlDescFlightLastIn\% in
a group. Two variants give the same picture. Giving the spanning transitions to the class where they
start, $\Delta_\mathrm{in}$ is \TlFlightStartDeltaIn{} \TlFlightStartDeltaInBand{} and $\Delta_{AB}$
\TlFlightStartDeltaAfterBefore{} \TlFlightStartDeltaAfterBeforeBand. Keeping only the stretches of at
least $\TlFactor\,x_\mathrm{min} = \TlXminLong$~s, they are \TlFlightLongDeltaIn{}
\TlFlightLongDeltaInBand{} and \TlFlightLongDeltaAfterBefore{} \TlFlightLongDeltaAfterBeforeBand.

\emph{Where the raw tail comes from.} Of the \TlRawGAbove{} transitions of G above the threshold,
\TlInGroupInRawG{} lie whole in a group stretch, \TlSpanningInRawG{} end after the stretch where they
start and \TlOutsideInRawG{} start before the first stretch of their flight (the sum differs by one
because the longest durations are cut in each pool). The spanning transitions
have $\hat\mu = \TlFlightMuSpanning{}$ \TlFlightMuSpanningBand, and the transitions whole in a group
stretch \TlFlightMuIn, close to \TlRawMuA{} for A and \TlRawMuS{} for S. A transition whole in a
stretch cannot outlast it, and group stretches are short: their median is \TlStretchMedianIn~s and
only \TlStretchLongShareIn\% last $\TlFactor\,x_\mathrm{min}$ or more. Part of the heavy tail of G
comes from transitions that start or end outside the group.

\emph{What a long transition is.} We describe the transitions above the threshold by their path, the
height lost in total, the height lost per km of net displacement and the share of their duration spent
in steps that gain height (Table~\ref{tab:descriptors}). Two readings of a heavier tail in a group are
open. Pilots in a group may dare more and commit to longer glides, which predicts long transitions that
spend more height in total. Or long transitions may follow lines of rising air that a group more often
flies, which predicts transitions that cost less height per km and rise a larger share of the time.
Raw, the long transitions of G are as long as those of S (path \TlDescRawPathG{} against
\TlDescRawPathS~km, difference \TlDescRawPathGapGS{} \TlDescRawPathGapGSBand~km), lose less height
and rise more. The numbers support the second reading. On the same route the transitions of the
solo controls cost as little as those of G, \TlDescRouteSinkSG{} against \TlDescRouteSinkG~m/km, and
the effect of the group at fixed route and pilot on the height lost per km is \TlDescRouteSinkGapD{}
\TlDescRouteSinkGapDBand~m/km. These bands rest on \TlDescRouteRunsG{} transitions of G, so they are
wide. The path is not longer in a group, and no group effect on the tail is established.

\section{Terrain}

We class the terrain by the landforms of Hammond~\cite{Hammond1964} in their computer version~\cite{Dikau1991}.
The elevation is the model BD ALTI of the French national geographic institute (IGN) at \TnSource~m,
averaged over blocks of $\TnBlock\times\TnBlock$ cells onto a grid of \TnCell~m. A block with more than
half of its cells without data has no elevation, and neither has a block whose cells all stand at 0~m,
which is sea (\TnMaskedShare\% of the blocks with an elevation). The slope is taken by central
differences on this grid, and a cell is gentle when its slope is below \TnGentle{} (rise over run). In a
square window of $\TnWindow\times\TnWindow$ cells (\TnWindowKm~km) around a cell we take the relief, the
maximum minus the minimum elevation, and the gentle share. A window with fewer than \TnCoverage{} of
valid cells gives the class unknown. Otherwise the cell is plains if the relief is below
\TnPlainsRelief~m and the gentle share above \TnPlainsGentle, hills if the relief is below
\TnHillsRelief~m and it is not plains, low mountains from \TnHillsRelief{} to \TnLowRelief~m and high
mountains from \TnLowRelief~m up.

A piece takes the class of the cell under its grid points when at least \TnMajority\% of them are in
it, and is mixed otherwise (\TnPiecesMixedG{} pieces of G), or unknown when its points have no class
(\TnPiecesUnknownG). An event of the same-flight design takes a class when its four windows share it.
Plains hold few pieces (\TnPiecesPlainsG{} pieces of G against \TnPiecesHillsG{} in the hills), so
we join plains and hills as lowland. Of the grid points of G, \TnShareLowlandG\% lie over the lowland
and \TnShareMountainG\% over the mountains, against \TnShareLowlandS\% and \TnShareMountainS\% for S.
Table~\ref{tab:terrain} gives $\Delta\Hloc(\LtLagOne~\mathrm{s})$ by class. Alone, a pilot over the lowland
already flies nearly straight, $\Hloc(\LtLagOne~\mathrm{s}) = \TnRawLowlandHS$ against \TnRawHighHS{} over
high mountains, so that there is less to gain there.

Few pieces of G over the lowland find a control on the same route: \TnRouteLowlandPiecesG{} of
\TnRawLowlandPiecesG{} (\TnRouteMatchedShareLowland\%), against \TnRouteMountainPiecesG{} of
\TnRawMountainPiecesG{} over the mountains (\TnRouteMatchedShareMountain\%). We did not test why. The
class is that of the terrain under the piece and not of the day or of the pilot: the pieces over the
mountains come from many more pilots (\TnRawMountainPilotsG{} against \TnRawLowlandPilotsG), who also
differ in sites and in weather. We read three classes and two designs at once and make no correction
for it.

\section{Group structure}

\emph{Order.} In a group, the pilot ahead is the one that passes a place first. In groups of three
or more, one pilot is ahead for \StOneLeader\% of the time in which the ordering is acyclic, against
\StOneLeaderNull\% when the directions of the links are drawn at random. The pilot ahead changes
\StSwitches{} times per hour of contact, against \StSwitchesChance{} in chance contacts of the same
duration. In groups of two pilots with a known record, the leader has more earlier flights in the
catalogue in \StExp\% of \StExpPairs{} pairs and the higher wing class in \StWing\% of
\StWingPairs{} pairs.

\emph{Headings.} In glide, with $\theta_i(t)$ the heading of pilot $i$ and $\theta_\mathrm{route}$
the direction of the displacement of the centroid over the glide, let $\varphi_i = \theta_i -
\theta_\mathrm{route}$ wrapped to $(-\pi,\pi]$. For a pair with $i$ ahead,
\begin{equation}
C(\tau) = \operatorname{corr}_t\bigl(\varphi_i(t),\,\varphi_j(t+\tau)\bigr),
\end{equation}
over the instants of the glide where both exist, with $\tau > 0$ when $j$ follows $i$. A follower that
retraces the track of the leader peaks at $\tau$ equal to the delay $\ell$ along the route. A follower
that answers the turns of the leader peaks near $\tau = 0$ at every delay. For delays of \StHdLOne,
\StHdLTwo, \StHdLThree{} and \StHdLFour~s (median per class) the peaks of the pairs lie at
\StHdPeakOne, \StHdPeakTwo, \StHdPeakThree{} and \StHdPeakFour~s, and there is no second peak near
zero lag. The null model is made of surrogate pairs, two pilots who never saw each other
(\StHdChance{} against \StHdGlides{} pair-glides in groups).

\emph{Marking of places.} The start of a climb is marked when a glider outside the pilot's group is
in a climb within $R_\mathrm{m} = \StMkR$~m of it in the window of $T_\mathrm{m} = \StMkDT$~s before it.
Both are the \StMkPercentile th percentiles of the distance and of the delay between the entries of two
members of one thermal. Places keep producing climbs, so we shift the markers in time by $\pm s$ and
call $m(s)$ the share of marked starts. We read $s_{1/2}$, the shift at which $m$ falls to half of
$m(0)$, and the ratio $m(0)/m(s_\mathrm{n})$ with $s_\mathrm{n} = \StMkNear$~min, which says how much
a glider climbing right then adds over one that climbed there minutes before or after.

\emph{Encounters.} Two gliders not in contact that come close join when they enter a contact. A group
offers more gliders to meet, so it is joined more often without any attraction. With $p(d)$ the share
joined by two solo gliders at the closest distance $d$ and $nm$ the number of pairs of gliders of the
two units, the expected number of joins is
\begin{equation}
E = \sum_\mathrm{passes} \Bigl[ 1 - \bigl(1 - p(d_\mathrm{min})\bigr)^{nm} \Bigr] ,
\end{equation}
and we read the observed joins over $E$. Table~\ref{tab:structure} gives the values.

\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robust}

The gap of G against its matched controls, G $-$ S$|$G, keeps its sign over the \MatRobLines{} lines
of the match. They vary the caliper (narrower, and the level of the wing instead of its class), the
isolation of the piece, the radius and the cover. At \LtLagOne~s it stays between
\MatRobLowHK{} and \MatRobHighHK, with a band that includes 0 in \MatRobZeroHK{} of them. At \LtLagTwo~s,
on the \MatRobLinesHQ{} lines whose long series reaches that lag, it stays between \MatRobLowHQ{} and
\MatRobHighHQ, with a band that includes 0 in \MatRobZeroHQ. The lines keep between \MatRobPiecesLow{}
and \MatRobPiecesHigh{} pieces. By level of the wing, the effect at fixed route and pilot at \LtLagOne~s is
\MatLevelBeginnersDHK{} for beginners (wings A and B) and \MatLevelExperiencedDHK{} for experienced
pilots (wings C, D and CCC), against \MatLevelAllDHK{} for all, as point values, which are alike at every level.
