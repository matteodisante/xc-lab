\section{Finding the groups}\label{app:groups}

Two flights are in contact when one passes where the other passes, within a delay
$\delta=\LtDeltaMin$~min before or after. Write $\mathbf{x}$ for the horizontal position, $z$ for
the altitude and $\mathbf{h}$ for the heading. A correspondence between flights $i$ and $j$ is a
pair of instants $(t,t')$ with
\begin{equation}
|t'-t|\le\delta,\quad
\|\mathbf{x}_i(t)-\mathbf{x}_j(t')\|<\varepsilon_h,\quad
|z_i(t)-z_j(t')|<\varepsilon_z,\quad
\angle\bigl(\mathbf{h}_i(t),\mathbf{h}_j(t')\bigr)<\theta ,
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_h=\LtEpsH$~m, $\varepsilon_z=\LtEpsZ$~m and $\theta=\LtTheta^\circ$. The heading
condition applies only when both pilots are in glide: in a climb or a search the heading turns.
The flights are in contact at $t$ when $t$ belongs to a correspondence $(t,t')$ or $(t',t)$.
Interruptions shorter than \LtGapMin~min are bridged, and a contact counts when it lasts at least
$\tau^*$.

We estimate chance with surrogate encounters. On each date we draw pairs of flights $(i,j)$ with
takeoffs within \LtNullRadiusKm~km and replace $j$ by a copy shifted in time,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}^s_j(t)=\mathbf{x}_j(t+r),\qquad r=(t^0_j-t^0_i)-\vartheta ,
\end{equation}
and likewise for $z$. Here $t^0$ is the takeoff time and $\vartheta$ is drawn from the takeoff-time
differences of the pairs of that date, so the copy takes off $\vartheta$ after $i$, as a real pilot
could. We keep only shifts with $|r|>2\delta$ plus one grid step, so no real correspondence
survives.

Let $N(\tau)$ be the number of real contacts lasting at least $\tau$ and $C(\tau)$ the number that
chance gives the same pairs: the surrogate contacts lasting at least $\tau$, per second of exposure,
scaled date by date to the exposure of the real pairs. We chose $\tau^*$ as the shortest $\tau$ with
$C(\tau)/N(\tau)\le\LtNullShare\%$. It is $\tau^*=\tauStar$~min.

At each instant the flights in the air are the nodes of a graph whose edges are the contacts. Its
connected components of two or more flights are the configurations. A group is a set of pilots that
are the members of a configuration over a maximal interval: a pilot who joins or leaves ends it.
An episode is a chain of groups, each starting when the previous one ends and keeping at least one
member.

In the data, \LtGroupFlights{} flights fly in a group at some point, for \LtGroupHours{}
flight-hours.
