\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Group time differs from solo flight, but so do the pilots, the places, the routes and the shape of the
flights. This report compares paragliders in three populations, solo flights, group time and the same
flights alone, and gives for each result the number of flights or pieces behind it. Flights declared open
fold back on themselves, and their shape enters the mean squared displacement: we keep those whose
landing is at least \FoldMinOpening{} of the path flown from the takeoff. On them the three populations
progress almost alike, $\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ of \HlocThousandG{} in group time against \HlocThousandS{}
in solo flights, up to lags of hours; group time is straighter only in the larger groups, and it climbs
more and glides faster and flatter. Against the same route flown on another day by isolated solo flights of
the same wing level, on the declared open flights, the gap $\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ falls from
\IsoBaseDHloc{} to \IsoDHloc{} \IsoDHlocBand{} on \IsoPieces{} pieces: the route explains \IsoExplained\% of it
(\IsoExplainedExp\% for experienced pilots, \IsoExplainedBeg\% for beginners). What remains is small, and
whether the group causes it stays open.
\end{abstract}
