\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Group time differs from solo flight, but so do the pilots, the places and the routes of groups. This
report compares paragliders in three populations, solo flights, group time and the same flights
alone, and gives for each result the number of flights or pieces behind it. Raw, group time progresses
straighter over a quarter of an hour than solo flights, $\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ of \HlocThousandG{}
against \HlocThousandS{}, and glides faster and flatter. On the long pieces group time keeps this
progress to lags of hours, and it is straighter with more pilots in the group. The ordering holds in
\RegionOrdered{} of the \RegionZones{} zones of \RegionCell~km with the most group flights. Against the same
route flown on another day by isolated solo flights of the same wing level, the gap
$\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ falls from \IsoBaseDHloc{} to \IsoDHloc{} \IsoDHlocBand{} on \IsoPieces{} pieces:
the route explains \IsoExplained\% of it, for experienced pilots and beginners alike. What remains is small
and goes with flying together; whether the group causes it stays open.
\end{abstract}
