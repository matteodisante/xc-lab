\section{Conclusions and limits}\label{sec:conclusions}

What can be concluded, and what cannot?

\paragraph{What we keep.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Raw, group time ($\G$, \PopFlightsG{} flights) progresses straighter than solo flights
        ($\Sl$, \PopFlightsS{}) over long lags, and the same flights alone ($\A$, \PopFlightsA{}) are no
        straighter than solo flights.
  \item On the long pieces group time keeps its progress to lags of hours, at every size of the group.
        $\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ is highest in group time, then in the same flights alone, then in solo
        flights in \RegionOrdered{} of the \RegionZones{} zones with the most group flights, where pooled the
        same flights alone are below solo flights.
  \item The route, with the season, the hour and the wing level, explains \IsoExplained\% of the gap
        $\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ between group time and solo flight: \IsoExplainedExp\% for experienced
        pilots, \IsoExplainedBeg\% for beginners.
  \item What remains, \IsoDHloc{} \IsoDHlocBand{}, is small, with a band away from zero; at short lags group time is less
        straight than the control.
  \item On the same route no statistic of climbs and glides differs from the control beyond its band.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limits.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The raw comparison fixes nothing: $\Sl$ and $\G$ differ in pilots, places, days and hours.
  \item The days differ: season and hour are matched, the weather of the day is not.
  \item The controls flew with nobody within 10~km. They may differ from the pilots who fly in
        groups, and this test has no selection contrast to measure it.
  \item Only the \IsoPieces{} pieces whose route was flown alone on another day enter; their routes
        are frequented ones.
  \item The bands measure the sampling of pilots and blocks. The radius and the cover of the route
        were varied only in the general report, on a sample, and the isolation distance not at all.
\end{itemize}
Fixing the route removes most of the straighter progress of groups; whether what remains is caused by
the group stays open.
