\section{Conclusions and limits}\label{sec:conclusions}

What can be concluded, and what cannot?

\paragraph{What we keep.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Declared open flights fold back on themselves; we keep the straight ones, with an opening of at
        least \FoldMinOpening, so that the shape of the flights does not pass for an effect of the group.
  \item On straight open flights group time ($\G$, \PopFlightsG{} flights), solo flights ($\Sl$,
        \PopFlightsS{}) and the same flights alone ($\A$, \PopFlightsA{}) progress almost alike, up to lags
        of hours; group time is straighter in the larger groups. Group time climbs more and glides faster and
        flatter. Zone by zone the order of the three populations changes.
  \item On the declared open flights, the route, with the season, the hour and the wing level, explains
        \IsoExplained\% of the gap $\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ between group time and solo flights
        (\IsoExplainedExp\% for experienced pilots, \IsoExplainedBeg\% for beginners). What remains,
        \IsoDHloc{} \IsoDHlocBand, is small; no statistic of climbs and glides differs from the control
        beyond its band.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limits.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The raw comparison fixes nothing: $\Sl$ and $\G$ differ in pilots, places, days and hours.
  \item The days differ: season and hour are matched, the weather of the day is not.
  \item The controls flew with nobody within 10~km. They may differ from the pilots who fly in
        groups, and this test has no selection contrast to measure it.
  \item Only the \IsoPieces{} pieces whose route was flown alone on another day enter; their routes
        are frequented ones. On the straight open flights too few pieces have a control, so the route test
        rests on the declared open flights.
  \item The bands measure the sampling of pilots and blocks. The radius and the cover of the route
        were varied only in the general report, on a sample, and the isolation distance not at all.
\end{itemize}
Most of the straighter progress of group time comes from the shape of the flights and from the route;
what remains is small, and whether the group causes it stays open.
