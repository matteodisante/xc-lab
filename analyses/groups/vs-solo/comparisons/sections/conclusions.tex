\begin{itemize}
  \item Many flights declared open fold back on themselves, and the longest lags rest on those. An opening above \MinOpening{} drops the ones that come back near their takeoff and keeps nearly the same share of lowland and mountain flights.
  \item \textbf{Raw.} On the open flights group time progresses straighter than solo flight at the long lags: $\Hk$ \RawHKOneG{} in $\G$, \RawHKOneS{} in $\Sl$, \RawHKOneA{} in $\A$, a gap $\G-\Sl$ of \RawGapHK{} \RawGapHKBand; at the short lags it is a little less straight.
  It has more time in climb (\RawStatClimbG\% against \RawStatClimbS\%) and a faster glide (\RawStatGlideG{} against \RawStatGlideS~km/h); the bands of the gaps exclude 0 for \RawNonZeroCount{} of the 7 statistics.
  \item \textbf{Same route, season, hour and wing.} The gap of $\Hk$ is \MatDeltaHKG{} \MatDeltaHKGBand, a band that excludes 0; that of $\Hq$ is \MatDeltaHQG{} \MatDeltaHQGBand, and $\Hloc$ at 1000~s on the longer pieces \MatDeltaHKLongG{} \MatDeltaHKLongGBand. Group time climbs more (\MatDeltaClimbG{} points of time in climb)
  and glides slower and steeper than its controls. The bands include 0 for \MatZeroG{} of the \MatNQuant{} quantities, and those of the selection term $\Delta(\A-\SAA)$ for \MatZeroA{} of them: on the same route the pilots who fly in groups do not differ from their controls when alone, and their group time does at the long lags.
  \item \textbf{Decomposition.} The route carries the largest part of $\Delta(\emptyset)$ for time in climb, glide speed, height lost per km of glide and $\Hq$; season and hour carry almost none, and the wing a small part. $\Delta(\emptyset)$ differs from the raw gap
  because of the pieces, the controls without a route and the unit. At $\Hk$ it is near 0 and no share is given.
  \item \textbf{Before and after.} The drift-free effect $g$ of $\Hk$ is \PreGHK{} \PreGHKBand, above the band of the same-route gap on dataset 1000 and close to the same-route gap at 1000~s on the longer pieces (\MatDeltaHKLongG). Time in climb, searches ending in a climb and climbs per hour
  agree in sign with the same-route gap. Time in search, climb rate, glide speed and height lost per km of glide have the opposite sign; for the climb rate, the glide speed and the height lost both bands exclude 0. We give no reason for it.
\end{itemize}
