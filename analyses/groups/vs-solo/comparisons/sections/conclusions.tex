\section{What we keep}\label{sec:conclusions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Many flights declared open fold back on themselves, and the longest lags rest on those: the mean
        squared displacement then bends with the shape of the flights, not with the group.
  \item An opening above \MinOpening{} drops the declared open flights that come back near their takeoff
        and keeps nearly the same share of lowland and mountain flights; a stricter threshold would select
        the region.
  \item On the open flights, raw, group time ($\G$, \OpenFlightsG{} flights) progresses straighter than solo
        flights ($\Sl$, \OpenFlightsS) up to lags of hours, and the same flights alone ($\A$, \OpenFlightsA)
        move like solo flights.
\end{itemize}

The comparison is raw: group and solo flights differ in route, season, hour and wing, which the next
comparisons fix.
