\section{Aim}\label{sec:aim}

Does a paraglider move differently in a group than alone? This report compares group time with solo flight on
open flights in four ways, each on the same populations and measures.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Raw} (Section~\ref{sec:raw}): $\Sl$, $\A$ and $\G$ as they are.
  \item \textbf{Same route} (Section~\ref{sec:matched}): pieces of group time against solo segments on the same route, in the same season and hour, with the same wing.
  \item \textbf{Decomposition} (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition}): how much of the raw gap the route, the season and hour, and the wing each account for.
  \item \textbf{Before, during, after} (Section~\ref{sec:prepost}): the same flight in the windows around its group time.
\end{enumerate}

The four comparisons answer three questions in turn. Does group time differ from solo flight (raw)? Is it the group, or what goes with it: the route, season, hour, wing and who flies in groups (same route and decomposition)? Does a flight change when it enters and leaves a group, which keeps who flies fixed (before, during, after)?

Section~\ref{sec:flights} defines the flights, the populations and the measures; Sections~\ref{sec:declared} and
\ref{sec:opening} say which flights are open and why. Every table and figure gives the flights or pieces it rests on,
its dataset and its estimator. Appendices give the formulas of each tool.
