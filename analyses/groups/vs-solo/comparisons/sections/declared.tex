On the dataset up to \DeclTauFixed~s group time is the straightest (Figure~\ref{fig:declared}):
$\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ is \DeclFixedHThousandG{} in $\G$, against \DeclFixedHThousandS{} in $\Sl$ and
\DeclFixedHThousandA{} in $\A$, and the fitted $H$ is \DeclFixedFitG, \DeclFixedFitS{} and \DeclFixedFitA. On the
dataset up to \DeclTauLongest~s the mean squared displacement of $\Sl$ and $\A$ bends down at lags of hours:
at \DeclTauLongest~s $\Hloc$ is \DeclLongestHS{} in $\Sl$ and \DeclLongestHA{} in $\A$. That of $\G$ stays at
\DeclLongestHG{} \DeclLongestHGBand, on \DeclLongestFlightsG{} flights only; the fitted $H$ is \DeclLongestFitG,
\DeclLongestFitS{} and \DeclLongestFitA.

\paragraph{What we keep.} Raw, group time progresses straighter than solo flights and than the same flights
alone, and at lags of hours the motion of solo flights and of the same flights alone bends away from a
power law.
