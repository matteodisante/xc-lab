Up to 1000~s group time is the straightest: $\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ is \DeclHThousandG{} in $\G$, against
\DeclHThousandS{} in $\Sl$ and \DeclHThousandA{} in $\A$ (Figure~\ref{fig:declared}). On the dataset up to
\DeclTauFixed~s the order is the same: at \DeclTauFixed~s, \DeclFixedHG{} \DeclFixedHGBand{} in $\G$,
\DeclFixedHS{} in $\Sl$ and \DeclFixedHA{} in $\A$. On the dataset up to \DeclTauLongest~s the mean squared
displacement of $\Sl$ and $\A$ bends down beyond about $5\cdot10^3$~s: at \DeclTauLongest~s $\Hloc$ is
\DeclLongestHS{} in $\Sl$ and \DeclLongestHA{} in $\A$. That of $\G$ stays at \DeclLongestHG{}
\DeclLongestHGBand, on \DeclLongestFlightsG{} flights only.

\paragraph{What we keep.} Raw, group time progresses straighter than solo flights and than the same flights
alone, and at lags of hours the motion of solo flights and of the same flights alone bends away from a
power law.
