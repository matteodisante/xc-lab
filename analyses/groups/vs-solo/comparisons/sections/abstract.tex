\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We compare paraglider group time with solo flights and with the solo time of the same flights, on the
flights the pilots declare open on the FFVL catalogue, with no stratification. At lags of hours the mean
squared displacement of solo flights bends down: the longest lags rest on declared open flights that fold
back on themselves, whose takeoff to landing distance is a small part of the path flown. We keep the
declared open flights with an opening above \MinOpening, the lowest threshold that drops those coming back
near their takeoff while keeping nearly the same share of lowland and mountain flights (\TakeoffLowKeep\%
and \TakeoffHighKeep\%; a threshold of \Stricter{} would keep \TakeoffLowStrict\% and \TakeoffHighStrict\%). On
these open flights group time progresses straighter than solo flights, $\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ of
\OpenFixedHThousandG{} against \OpenFixedHThousandS, the same flights alone stay close to solo flights, and the
bend at long lags is weaker.
\end{abstract}
