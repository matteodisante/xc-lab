\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We compare paraglider group time with solo flight on open flights, four ways: raw; against solo flights on the
same route, season, hour and wing; by how much each of those factors accounts for the raw gap; and against the
same flight before and after its group time. All bands come from a two-way bootstrap of pilots and blocks, whose 90\% coverage we check by simulation on the designs of the data.
Raw, group time progresses straighter than solo flight at the long lags: $\Hk$ is \RawHKOneG{} in $\G$ and \RawHKOneS{} in $\Sl$, a gap of \RawGapHK{} \RawGapHKBand.
On the same route, season, hour and wing the gap of $\Hk$ falls to \MatDeltaHKG{} \MatDeltaHKGBand{} on pieces of at least \RawShortestOne~s, while on longer pieces $\Hq$ gives \MatDeltaHQG{} \MatDeltaHQGBand; group time also climbs more often and glides slower and steeper than its controls, and at $\Hk$ the same pilots alone do not differ from theirs; at $\Hq$ they show \MatDeltaHQA{} \MatDeltaHQABand, as much as the group, so on the same route the group effect cannot be told from who flies in groups.
Taking the factors apart, the route carries the largest part of the raw differences of time in climb, glide speed and height lost per km of glide. Around the group, the
drift-free effect $g$ of $\Hk$ is \PreGHK{} \PreGHKBand, close to the same-route gap on the longer pieces, and the same for windows from \PreWinBLen{} to \PreWinDLen~s. This design keeps the same flight, so it does not depend on who flies. Time in search, climb rate, glide speed and height lost per km of glide have opposite signs in the matched and in the before-and-after comparison.
\end{abstract}
