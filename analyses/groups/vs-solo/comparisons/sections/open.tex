Group time is the straightest on both datasets (Figure~\ref{fig:open}): on the one up to \OpenTauFixed~s,
$\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ is \OpenFixedHThousandG{} in $\G$, against \OpenFixedHThousandS{} in $\Sl$ and
\OpenFixedHThousandA{} in $\A$, and the fitted $H$ is \OpenFixedFitG, \OpenFixedFitS{} and \OpenFixedFitA. The
same flights alone stay close to solo flights. The bend at long lags is weaker than on the declared open
flights: at \OpenTauLongest~s $\Hloc$ is \OpenLongestHS{} in $\Sl$ and \OpenLongestHA{} in $\A$, against
\DeclLongestHS{} and \DeclLongestHA{} before the threshold. That of $\G$ is \OpenLongestHG{}
\OpenLongestHGBand, on \OpenLongestFlightsG{} flights only; the fitted $H$ is \OpenLongestFitG, \OpenLongestFitS{}
and \OpenLongestFitA.

\paragraph{What we keep.} On open flights, raw, group time progresses straighter than solo flights on both
datasets, while the same flights alone stay close to solo flights; the threshold weakens the bend at lags of hours.
