\section{The raw difference}\label{sec:raw}

How do group time and solo flights differ before any control?

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{report_raw.pdf}
\caption{Pooled statistics of $\Sl$, $\G$ and $\A$ (paragliders), with 5--95\% bands. Exponents on
loggers of at most 10~s, phases on 1~Hz flights. Values in Table~\ref{tab:raw}.}
\label{fig:raw}
\end{figure}

Group time progresses straighter over long lags than solo flights: $\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ is
\HlocThousandG{} in group time against \HlocThousandS{} in solo flights. It also spends more time
climbing, ends more searches in a climb, glides faster and glides flatter, with a glide ratio of
\RatioG{} against \RatioS{} (Figure~\ref{fig:raw}; the curves over every lag are in
Appendix~\ref{app:curves}). The same flights alone sit between the two at long lags
(\HlocThousandA) and have the highest climb rate. Part of the raw difference may therefore belong to
who flies in groups, and to where and when they fly.

\paragraph{What we keep.} A difference to explain, largest at long lags and in the glides. The next
three sections ask how much of it is the group.
