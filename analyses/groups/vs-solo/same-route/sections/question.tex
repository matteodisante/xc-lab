\section{Group or route?}\label{sec:question}

Over a quarter of an hour, group time progresses straighter than solo flight. The general report
(\texttt{vs-solo/\allowbreak general}) measures $\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ of \IsoBaseDHloc{} against the solo
flights of the same site, day and hour. That comparison leaves the route free. Groups may form on a
ridge or a convergence line where any pilot progresses straight, and the straightness would then
belong to the route and not to the group. Does the gap remain when group time and its control fly the
same route?

This report answers with the same route flown alone, on another day. It differs from the test of the
general report on three points.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No site condition, all the pieces.} The control may come from any site and date other
        than that of the piece, and every piece of group time of the paragliders that has one is used,
        not a sample.
  \item \textbf{Controls that were really alone.} A flight with no contact in the grouping can still
        have flown near others, so the control must also have had nobody within 10~km at the same
        time.
  \item \textbf{By wing level.} A piece is compared with controls of the same level, and the
        results are given for experienced and beginner pilots apart.
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:matching} says how a control is chosen and how the gap is measured,
Section~\ref{sec:gap} gives the gap, Section~\ref{sec:levels} the gap by wing level and
Section~\ref{sec:phases} the climbs and glides.
