\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Over a quarter of an hour a paraglider in a group progresses straighter than a solo one, but groups
form on routes where any pilot may progress straight. We compare each piece of group time with the
same route flown on another day, in the same season, hour and wing level, by flights that never had
a contact and that no other flight came within 10~km of at the same time. On \IsoPieces{} pieces the
gap $\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ is \IsoDHloc{} \IsoDHlocBand{} against these controls and
\IsoBaseDHloc{} against the solo flights of the same site, day and hour: fixing the route lowers it by
\IsoExplained\%, by \IsoExplainedExp\% for experienced pilots and \IsoExplainedBeg\% for beginners.
The rest is small and its band excludes zero. At 100~s group time is less straight ($\Delta\Hloc(100~\mathrm{s})$ is \IsoDHlocHundred),
and groups spend more time climbing and less gliding, while climb rate, glide speed and glide ratio do not
differ. The route accounts for most of the straighter progress; what remains goes with flying together,
and whether the group causes it stays open.
\end{abstract}
