\section{Data and measures}\label{sec:setup}

Which flights, which populations, and which measures of the motion?

\paragraph{Flights and groups.} We use \input{generated/n_flights} on 6\,209 days, the cleaned tracks
of the old repository (soaring, tag \texttt{legacy-final}). A flight is analysed after its
\emph{departure}, the first fix 3~km from the takeoff; before it the pilot counts as neither solo
nor in a group. The measures of the motion use loggers of at most 10~s; the phases (climb, search,
glide) are Vilpellet's runs and exist on the flights logged every second, about 70\% of the
paragliders. The groups are those of the note, with $\delta=2$~min, $\varepsilon_h=500$~m,
$\varepsilon_z=300$~m, $\theta_{\max}=45^\circ$, $g=2$~min and $q=0.1$, hence contacts of at least
$\tau^*=\tauStar$~min (its sections 3--6). A contact means shared air within sight; it does not
prove interaction.

\paragraph{What the grouping gives.} \input{generated/grouping_facts}

\paragraph{Populations.} Three populations of stretches of flight.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Sl$, \emph{solo flights}: flights never in a group.
  \item $\G$, \emph{group time}: the stretches of a flight inside a configuration, a fixed set of
        flights that stays one group over a maximal interval. $\G_n$ is the group time in
        configurations of $n$ pilots; ``$n$ pilots'' always means the size of the configuration at
        that instant.
  \item $\A$, \emph{the same flights alone}: the stretches of the group flights outside any
        configuration. Nearly every group flight has some (Table~\ref{tab:raw}), so $\A$ has nearly
        the pilots, gliders, loggers and days of $\G$, and serves as its control.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The motion over every lag.} The horizontal position $\mathbf r(t)$ of a flight is read on
a 10-s grid of its clock. A \emph{piece} is the part of a stretch within one segment of the track; it
enters only if it lasts at least 1250~s, so that every population keeps the same flights at all 21
lags from 10 to 1000~s. For a flight $f$ and a population, the time-averaged mean squared
displacement pools the origins of all its pieces in that population,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tamsd}
  \overline{\delta^2_f}(\tau)=\frac{1}{N_f(\tau)}\sum_{t}\bigl\|\mathbf r(t+\tau)-\mathbf r(t)\bigr\|^2 ,
\end{equation}
where $t$ runs over the $N_f(\tau)$ origins with $t$ and $t+\tau$ in the same piece, and the
ensemble $\MSD(\tau)$ is the mean of $\overline{\delta^2_f}(\tau)$ over the flights, one weight per
flight. With $\MSD(\tau)\propto\tau^{2H}$, $H$ is half the slope of $\log\MSD$ against $\log\tau$ over
all lags: $H=1$ for a straight line, $H=1/2$ for a random walk. $\Hloc(\tau)$ is the same slope over
the lags within $\pm0.25$ decades of $\tau$ (Appendix~\ref{app:hloc}). We read it at two scales:
$\tau=100$~s, within one climb or one glide, and $\tau=1000$~s (lags 580--1000~s), over one to two
climb--glide cycles.

\paragraph{Climbs and glides.} Each phase run goes whole to the population of the stretch that holds
its middle, and each statistic is a ratio of sums over the runs of a population, so long phases weigh
more. Of the twelve statistics measured, five are not explained by the others and are the ones drawn
(Appendix~\ref{app:redundancy}): the share of time in climb; the share of searches that end in a
climb; the climb rate, total height gained over total climb time; the glide speed, total
straight-line distance over total glide time; and the glide ratio, total distance over total height
lost, replaced in stratified contrasts by its inverse, the height lost per kilometre of glide. The
tables also give the cross-country speed, the straight-line distance covered in 30~min averaged over
every origin, which the five explain.

\paragraph{Strata.} A \emph{zone} is a cell of 25~km containing the middle of a piece; a
\emph{site-day} holds the flights of one day whose takeoffs lie within 3~km; the \emph{hour} is the
local hour of the middle of a piece or a run.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} Bands are 5--95\% of bootstrap draws that resample whole dependency blocks,
the flights tied by sharing episodes, and recompute the statistic: the blocks of the note for the
pooled values of Tables~\ref{tab:raw} and~\ref{tab:size} (1000 draws); blocks that also join the
flights of a site-day for every contrast (200 draws, the same for every statistic). Every difference
is computed draw by draw and has its own band. The bands measure the sampling of blocks; the choice of
$q$ is tested in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}.

\paragraph{What we keep.} Three populations, $\Sl$, $\G$ and $\A$; two scales of the motion,
$\Hloc(100~\mathrm{s})$ and $\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$; five statistics of climbs and glides; bands from
blocks of flights that are not independent.
