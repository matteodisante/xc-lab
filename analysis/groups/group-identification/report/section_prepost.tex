\section{Group or moment? The same flight before, during and after}\label{sec:prepost}

Do pilots fly together in the moments when the progress is straight anyway? If so, the same flight
would already be straight just before the group forms, and still just after it breaks.

\paragraph{Windows.} Every stretch of group time lasting at least $W=\PPWindowMin$~min is an
\emph{event}, with up to four windows of length $W$ on the clock of the same flight: \emph{before},
the last $W$ of the solo stretch that precedes it; \emph{start} and \emph{end}, the first and last $W$
of the group; \emph{after}, the first $W$ of the solo stretch that follows it. Before and after exist
only when a solo stretch at least $W$ long touches the group. For two windows the ensemble MSD is taken
on the events that have both, and their difference is read on $\Hloc$ and on the phase statistics.
The two readings predict different things:
\begin{itemize}
  \item if the straighter progress belongs to the group, $\Hloc$ rises from before to start, falls
        from end to after, and before and after are alike;
  \item if it belongs to the moment, before is already high, and the changes are those of any two
        consecutive windows.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Controls.} Two consecutive windows with no change of state, drawn in the solo time of the
group flights ($\A$) and in solo flights ($\Sl$), measure how much a flight drifts from one window to
the next.

\paragraph{Results.} The first prediction holds. $\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ is \PPEntryDHloc{}
\PPEntryDHlocBand{} entering and \PPExitDHloc{} \PPExitDHlocBand{} leaving, against \PPCtrlADHloc{} and
\PPCtrlSDHloc{} in the controls, and after minus before is \PPAfterBeforeDHloc{}
\PPAfterBeforeDHlocBand. On the \PPProfileEvents{} events with all four windows, $\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$
is \PPProfileHlocPre{} before, \PPProfileHlocStart{} and \PPProfileHlocEnd{} in the group and
\PPProfileHlocPost{} after (Figure~\ref{fig:prepost-profile}); the step matches the within-flight
estimate of Section~\ref{sec:other} (\DHlocThousandFlight). At 100~s the changes, \PPEntryDHlocHundred{}
entering and \PPExitDHlocHundred{} leaving, lie inside the drift of the controls (\PPCtrlADHlocHundred,
\PPCtrlSDHlocHundred). The fitted $H$ (\PPEntryDH{} and \PPExitDH{} against \PPCtrlADH{} and
\PPCtrlSDH) does not separate from the drift either, because its slope is set mostly by the lags
below 150~s. Glides are faster and flatter in the group in both directions: glide speed
\PPEntrySpeed{} km/h entering and \PPExitSpeed{} leaving, height lost per km \PPEntrySink{} and
\PPExitSink{} m/km, against \PPCtrlSSpeed{} and \PPCtrlASpeed{} km/h, \PPCtrlASink{} and \PPCtrlSSink{}
m/km in the controls. Time in climb, successful searches and climb rate move by the size of the drift.

\paragraph{Limits.} The windows fix the pilot, the glider, the day and the slow drift of the flight,
not what brings pilots together: a convergence line, a ridge or a common route may last as long as
the group and straighten the progress by itself, and two pilots must move alike to stay within
500~m for $W$. The events are long groups with solo flight beside them (\PPEntryEvents{} entries,
\PPExitEvents{} exits). With windows of \PPShortWindowMin~min the same test is noisier
(Appendix~\ref{app:prepost}).

\paragraph{What we keep.} On the same flight the straighter progress over a quarter of an hour starts
with the group and stops with it, and the glides change with it; the moment of joining does not
explain them.
