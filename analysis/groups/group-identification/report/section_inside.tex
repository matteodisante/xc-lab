\section{Inside the group: size and leader}\label{sec:inside}

Does the effect grow with the number of pilots, and does the pilot ahead fly differently from the one behind?

\subsection{Size of the configuration}\label{sec:size}

\textbf{Method.} The group time is cut where the configuration changes, so every stretch has a fixed number of pilots
$n$ (2, 3, 4, 5--6, 7+); a flight can contribute to several sizes. Two contrasts by size: each size minus the same
flights alone (within the flight), and each size minus configurations of 2 pilots at the same site-day and hour.

\begin{reading}
\textbf{Reading.} Pooled, $H$ and $H_\mathrm{loc}$ rise with $n$ while the same flights alone stay flat
(Fig.~\ref{fig:size-hloc}, Table~\ref{tab:size}), and within the flight the effect grows with $n$
(\DHlocThousandFlightTwo{} with 2 pilots, \DHlocThousandFlightSeven{} with 7+). At the same site-day and hour,
configurations of 3 to 6 pilots do not differ from pairs ($\Delta H_\mathrm{loc}(1000)$ between \DHlocThousandMiddleMin{}
and \DHlocThousandMiddleMax, bands across zero): the rise of the pooled curves belongs to the days and sites where large
groups form. Only 7+ stands apart (\DHlocThousandSevenTwoSdh{} \DHlocThousandSevenTwoSdhBand, cross-country speed
\DXcSevenTwoSdh{} km/h), a rare configuration (3\% of group time). Time in climb and the flatter glide do grow with $n$ at
the same place and hour (\DClimbSizeMin{} to \DClimbSizeMax{} points; \DSinkSizeMin{} to \DSinkSizeMax{} m/km).
\end{reading}

\subsection{Leader and follower}\label{sec:mechanisms}

\textbf{Method.} In configurations of two pilots, the signed median delay $\ell_{\rm med}$ of the pair interval (Appendix B
of the note) says who passes first: the leader, when the other passes at least 15~s later; otherwise the two fly
abreast (\Abreast\% of the pairs; median delay \DelayMedian~s). Leader and follower are compared within the same
configuration (Appendix~\ref{app:leaders}). How groups form and break and whether the same pilots lead are in section 7
of the note.

\begin{reading}
\textbf{Reading.} The pilot ahead flies a little faster (glide speed \LeadSpeed{} km/h, climb rate \LeadRate{} m/s) but
does not find thermals more often (searches ending in a climb \LeadFound{} points, height lost per km \LeadSink{} m/km):
the follower shows no sign of using the leader to find the next thermal.
\end{reading}

\textbf{What we keep.} Most of the effect is there with two pilots; beyond a pair, size adds climbing and flatter glides
but not straighter progress, except in the rare configurations of 7 or more. Leading changes speed, not the finding of
thermals.
