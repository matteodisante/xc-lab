\section{Inside the group: size and leader}\label{sec:inside}

Does the effect grow with the number of pilots, and does the pilot ahead fly differently from the one
behind?

\subsection{Size of the configuration}\label{sec:size}

The group time is cut where the configuration changes, so every stretch has a fixed number of pilots
$n$ (2, 3, 4, 5--6, 7+), and a flight can contribute to several sizes. Two contrasts of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:contrast} follow the size: $\G_n-\A$ within the flight, and $\G_n-\G_2$ at the same
site-day and hour, which asks whether a group does more than a pair.

Pooled, $H$ and $\Hloc$ rise with $n$ while the same flights alone stay flat
(Figure~\ref{fig:size-hloc}, Table~\ref{tab:size}), and within the flight the effect grows with $n$,
from \DHlocThousandFlightTwo{} with 2 pilots to \DHlocThousandFlightSeven{} with 7+. At the same
site-day and hour, however, configurations of 3 to 6 pilots do not differ from pairs:
$\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ lies between \DHlocThousandMiddleMin{} and \DHlocThousandMiddleMax, with
bands across zero (Figure~\ref{fig:size}). The rise of the pooled curves belongs to the days and
sites where large groups form. Only 7+ stands apart (\DHlocThousandSevenTwoSdh{}
\DHlocThousandSevenTwoSdhBand, cross-country speed \DXcSevenTwoSdh{} km/h), a rare configuration, 3\% of
the group time. Time in climb and the flatter glide do grow with $n$ at the same place and hour, from
\DClimbSizeMin{} to \DClimbSizeMax{} points and from \DSinkSizeMin{} to \DSinkSizeMax{} m/km.

\subsection{Leader and follower}\label{sec:mechanisms}

In configurations of two pilots, the signed median delay $\ell_{\rm med}$ of the pair interval
(Appendix~B of the note) says who passes first: the leader, when the other passes at least 15~s later;
otherwise the two fly abreast (\Abreast\% of the pairs; median delay \DelayMedian~s). Leader and
follower are compared within the same configuration, the configuration being the stratum of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:contrast} (Appendix~\ref{app:leaders}). The pilot ahead flies a little faster (glide
speed \LeadSpeed{} km/h, climb rate \LeadRate{} m/s) but does not find thermals more often (searches
ending in a climb \LeadFound{} points, height lost per km \LeadSink{} m/km): the follower shows no sign
of using the leader to find the next thermal. How groups form and break and whether the same pilots
lead are in section 7 of the note.

\paragraph{What we keep.} Most of the effect is there with two pilots. Beyond a pair, size adds
climbing and flatter glides but no straighter progress, except in the rare configurations of 7 or
more. Leading changes the speed and leaves the finding of thermals unchanged.
