\subsection{The place: zones, months and hours}\label{sec:zones}

\textbf{Question.} Do groups fly where, and when, any pilot would progress in a straight line? \textbf{Method.} Each
piece of track is assigned to a \emph{stratum}: zone (cell of 25~km of its middle), zone$\times$month, zone$\times$hour,
site-day$\times$hour. In every stratum with both populations the ensemble MSD of S (and of A) is \emph{reweighted to
the mix of G over the strata}: S then flies, in the statistic, where and when G flies. We compare $H_\mathrm{loc}(\tau)$
over every lag and the fitted $H$; ``as is'' is the same comparison without the reweighting, on the same strata (a
flight counts once per cell it crosses).

\begin{reading}
\textbf{Reading.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The gap opens above $\tau\approx150$~s.} $\Delta H_\mathrm{loc}$ between G and S is $\ZoneShortMin$ to
        $\ZoneShortMax$ from 20 to 140~s and \ZoneDHlocSNone{} at 1000~s, above one climb--glide cycle: the group acts on
        how cycles are chained, not on a single glide or climb.
  \item \textbf{The zone removes little.} Reweighting S to the cells of G lowers $\Delta H_\mathrm{loc}(1000)$ by
        \ZoneRedLocZone\% (\ZoneDHlocSupportZone{} $\to$ \ZoneDHlocSZone) and the fitted $\Delta H$ by \ZoneRedHZone\%; with
        the month, \ZoneRedLocZoneMonth\% and \ZoneRedHZoneMonth\%; with the hour, \ZoneRedLocZoneHour\% and
        \ZoneRedHZoneHour\%. Solo pilots fly where groups fly: \ZoneKeptZone\% of G has a solo counterpart in its cell.
  \item \textbf{Same site-day and hour} (\ZoneKeptSdh\% of G kept): $\Delta H_\mathrm{loc}(1000)=\ZoneDHlocSSdh$
        \ZoneDHlocSSdhBand, \ZoneRedLocSdh\% below its as-is value; fitted $\Delta H=\ZoneDHSSdh$ \ZoneDHSSdhBand. The
        fitted $H$ of S rises by a few thousandths when reweighted (\ZoneHNoneS{} $\to$ \ZoneHSdhS) and never reaches that
        of G (\ZoneHSdhG).
  \item \textbf{Against the same flights alone:} $\Delta H_\mathrm{loc}(1000)$ is \ZoneDHlocANone{} pooled and
        \ZoneDHlocAZone, \ZoneDHlocAZoneHour, \ZoneDHlocASdh{} in zone, zone$\times$hour and site-day$\times$hour strata.
        Below 150~s the sign of $\G-\A$ depends on the hour, which is why the two estimates of
        Section~\ref{sec:other} are read together.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Limits.} Cells are 25~km, from the middle of a piece; the terrain inside a cell is not controlled, and a group
may choose better air within it: this is part of what we call the group effect. Paragliders only.
\end{reading}

\textbf{What we keep.} The place and the hour where groups fly explain at most a fifth of the gap; the rest stays at the
same site, day and hour.
