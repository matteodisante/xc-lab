\section{Group or pilots? Contrasts within strata}\label{sec:other}

How much of the difference belongs to who flies in groups, and how much to the group, with the day,
the site and the hour fixed?

\paragraph{Contrast.} For a statistic $X$ and two populations $P$ and $Q$, each stratum $s$ that holds
both gives $D_s=X_{P,s}-X_{Q,s}$, computed as on the whole archive (ensemble MSD, ratios of sums).
The contrast is their weighted mean,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contrast}
  \Delta_{P-Q}=\frac{\sum_s w_s D_s}{\sum_s w_s},\qquad w_s=\frac{n_{P,s}\,n_{Q,s}}{n_{P,s}+n_{Q,s}},
\end{equation}
with $n_{P,s}$ and $n_{Q,s}$ the flights of each population in $s$. The weight is half the harmonic
mean of the two counts: a stratum counts as much as its smaller side allows. For the exponents,
$D_s$ is the difference of $\log\MSD$ at every lag, and $\Delta H$ and $\Delta\Hloc$ are the slopes of
its weighted mean.

\paragraph{Five contrasts.} Each fixes something more (Table~\ref{tab:decompose}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\G-\Sl$ pooled: the raw difference;
  \item $\G-\Sl$ within site-day and hour: the same day, site and hour;
  \item $\A-\Sl$ within site-day and hour: \emph{selection}, how the flights that will fly in a group
        differ when they fly alone;
  \item $\G-\A$ within the flight, the stratum being one flight: pilot, glider, logger, day and site
        fixed;
  \item $\G-\A$ within site-day and hour: the hour fixed as well.
\end{itemize}
The last two estimate the group effect and fail in opposite ways. Within the flight, group time
falls around midday and the time alone of the same flight earlier and later (section 7 of the
note), so the hour differs. Within site-day and hour, the hour is the same but the pilots differ.
Where the two agree, the effect belongs to the group.

\paragraph{Results.} The straighter progress over 1000~s survives every control:
$\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})$ is \DHlocThousandRaw{} pooled, \DHlocThousandFlight{} within the flight
and \DHlocThousandGroupSdh{} at the same site-day and hour, while selection gives \DHlocThousandSel.
Within one phase nothing remains: at 100~s, \DHlocHundredFlight{} within the flight and
\DHlocHundredGroupSdh{} at the same site-day and hour; the pooled \DHlocHundredRaw{} was selection and
composition. In a group pilots climb more (\DClimbFlight{} and \DClimbGroupSdh{} points of time in
climb), end more searches in a climb (\DFoundFlight{} and \DFoundGroupSdh{} points) and climb faster
(\DRateFlight{} and \DRateGroupSdh{} m/s). Pooled, the climb rate of $\G$ is below that of $\A$: the
pooled ratios weight flights by their time in each population, and the strata undo that. Glides are
faster, \DSpeedFlight{} and \DSpeedGroupSdh{} km/h, and flatter, \DSinkFlight{} and \DSinkGroupSdh{} m/km
of height lost per km; selection adds \DSpeedSel{} km/h of glide speed, so group pilots glide faster
even alone. The cross-country speed rises by \DXcFlight{} and \DXcGroupSdh{} km/h.

\paragraph{What we keep.} With the pilot, the day, the site and the hour fixed, a group progresses
straighter over a quarter of an hour, climbs more and glides faster and flatter. Who flies in groups
explains little of it.
