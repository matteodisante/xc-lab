\section{Group or pilots? Contrasts within strata}\label{sec:other}

How much of the difference belongs to who flies in groups, and how much to the group, once the day,
the site and the hour are fixed?

\paragraph{Contrast.} For a statistic $X$ and two populations $P$ and $Q$, each stratum $s$ that holds
both gives $D_s=X_{P,s}-X_{Q,s}$, computed as on the whole archive (ensemble MSD, ratios of sums).
The contrast is their weighted mean,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contrast}
  \Delta_{P-Q}=\frac{\sum_s w_s D_s}{\sum_s w_s},\qquad w_s=\frac{n_{P,s}\,n_{Q,s}}{n_{P,s}+n_{Q,s}},
\end{equation}
with $n_{P,s}$ and $n_{Q,s}$ the flights of each population in $s$. The weight is half the harmonic
mean of the two counts: a stratum counts as much as its smaller side allows. For the exponents,
$D_s$ is the difference of $\log\MSD$ at every lag, and $\Delta H$ and $\Delta\Hloc$ are half the
slopes of its weighted mean, over all lags and within $\pm0.25$ decades.

\paragraph{Five contrasts} (Figure~\ref{fig:decompose}; values with bands in
Table~\ref{tab:decompose}, Appendix~\ref{app:tables}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\G-\Sl$ pooled: the raw difference;
  \item $\G-\Sl$ within site-day and hour: day, site and hour fixed;
  \item $\A-\Sl$ within site-day and hour: \emph{selection}, how the flights that fly in groups differ
        from solo flights when they fly alone;
  \item $\G-\A$ within the flight, the stratum being one flight: pilot, glider, logger, day and site
        fixed, the hour free;
  \item $\G-\A$ within site-day and hour: day, site and hour fixed, the pilots different.
\end{itemize}
The last two estimate the group effect and fail in opposite ways. Within the flight, group time falls
around midday and the time alone of the same flight earlier and later (section 7 of the note), so the
hour differs; within site-day and hour, the hour is the same but the pilots differ. Where the two
agree, neither the hour nor the pilot explains the difference.

\paragraph{Results} (Figure~\ref{fig:decompose}). The straighter progress over 1000~s survives both
estimates of the group effect, $\Delta\Hloc(1000~\mathrm{s})=$ \DHlocThousandFlight{} within the flight
and \DHlocThousandGroupSdh{} at the same site-day and hour, while selection gives \DHlocThousandSel. At
100~s the two estimates disagree in sign: within the flight the hour differs, and fixing it removes
the difference (Figure~\ref{fig:zones-diff}, right). In a group pilots spend more time climbing, end
more searches in a climb, climb faster, and glide faster and flatter, in both estimates; pooled, $\G$
climbs slower than $\A$ (Figure~\ref{fig:raw}), within strata faster. For these statistics the
within-flight values are two to three times the site-day-and-hour ones, as expected if the hour adds
to them, so the latter are the conservative estimates. Selection is small for the motion and the
climbs, and over half of the faster glides: group pilots glide faster even alone.

\paragraph{What we keep.} With the pilot fixed, or with the day, site and hour fixed, group time
progresses straighter over a quarter of an hour, climbs more and glides faster and flatter. Who flies
in groups explains little of the straighter progress and over half of the faster glides.
