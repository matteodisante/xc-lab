\section{Algorithm}\label{sec:algorithm}

\algrenewcommand\algorithmicrequire{\textbf{Input:}}
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicensure{\textbf{Output:}}

How can the definitions of Sections~\ref{sec:contact} and~\ref{sec:groups} be computed on the
whole archive? Literally, for every pair of flights of a day, for every instant of one and every
delay up to $\delta$, one would test the four conditions of~\eqref{eq:correspondences}. On a day
with 200 flights in the air this is $200\cdot199/2=19\,900$ pairs; with 1.5~h of flight on the
5-s grid, 1\,080 instants, and the 49 delays of $\delta=2$~min, it is about $10^9$ tests for that
day alone. Almost all of them are wasted: most pairs of the day are never within several
kilometres of each other. The algorithm therefore works in phases (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}): a
common time grid (Phase~0); a cheap test that discards, provably losing nothing, the pairs and
instants without a correspondence (Phase~1); the exact test on what remains (Phase~2); the
intervals (Phase~3); and a sweep over time that builds the groups (Phase~4).

\subsection{How a run is organised}\label{sec:run}

Flights of different days never meet, so every phase but the calibration works on one date at a
time: the dates run in parallel, and the memory needed is that of one day. A run has three
stages.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Per date.} Phase~0 puts the flights of the date on the grid; Phases~1--3 find the
        contact intervals of the real pairs, with their duration and $\ell_{\rm med}$, but
        without the threshold $\tau^*$, which is not known yet. The null model of
        Section~\ref{sec:null} draws the surrogate pairs of the date and sends each one through
        the same Phases~1--3, with the copy of $j$ shifted in time and filtered against $i$
        alone. The result of each date is saved, so an interrupted run resumes.
  \item \textbf{Once.} $\tau^*$ follows from the real and the surrogate intervals of all the
        dates, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fdr}.
  \item \textbf{Per date.} Phase~4 keeps the intervals lasting at least $\tau^*$ and builds
        groups, configurations, episodes and blocks.
\end{enumerate}
On the whole archive, 6\,209 dates, a run takes about an hour with five processes.

\subsection{Phase 0: preparation per flight}\label{sec:phase0}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Positions.} The Earth-centred position of every fix (Section~\ref{sec:data}). One
frame serves all the flights, at any distance and without a map projection, so the coordinates of
two flights compare directly.

\item \textbf{Altitude.} GNSS $z$, unchanged.

\item \textbf{Departure.} $k^\dep_i$ from~\eqref{eq:departure}.

\item \textbf{States and heading.} $s_i(k)$ from the phase labelling. In glide, $\vh_i(k)$ is the
direction of the horizontal velocity, filtered with Savitzky--Golay, \fix{in the east and north
of the first fix of the flight (the frame of the cleaned tracks) instead of those of the fix
itself. So two flights are compared in frames whose axes differ by the rotation of the local
north between their first fixes and the points compared: $(d/R)\tan\varphi$ for a displacement
$d$ to the east, about $3^\circ$ for 300~km at latitude $45^\circ$, small against
$\theta_{\max}=45^\circ$.}

\item \textbf{Grid.} The instants of $\Delta\Z$, with $\Delta=5$~s, the same for all flights. A
grid instant takes the position and the altitude of the fix at that instant or, between two fixes
of the same segment at most $h_{\max}=10$~s apart, the values interpolated linearly between them,
with the state and the heading of the nearer fix; elsewhere the flight has no position. On a
logger that records every second every grid instant is a fix. An interpolated position is at most
$v_{\max}h/2$ from the true one, for two fixes $h$ apart: 69~m at 5~s, 139~m at 10~s.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{doubt}{Is $\Delta=5$~s enough in a thermal?}
Four points per turn do not draw the spiral (Figure~\ref{fig:decimation}), but the contact does
not need its shape. What matters is how far a grid position can be from the track between two
grid instants, compared with the tolerances:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item horizontally, a pilot is never more than $v_{\max}\Delta/2\approx69$~m from the nearest
        grid point in the worst case, about 20~m while circling at 8~m/s; $\varepsilon_h=500$~m,
        and a whole thermal of radius 25~m fits in a small fraction of it;
  \item vertically, $w\Delta/2=5$~m at 2~m/s and at most 12.5~m at 5~m/s, against
        $\varepsilon_z=300$~m;
  \item on the delay, a grid of 5~s against $\delta=120$~s; in a thermal $l^*$ is undetermined
        over the whole window of accepted delays anyway (Appendix~\ref{sec:lag});
  \item on the ends of the intervals, $\pm\Delta$ against $g=120$~s.
\end{itemize}
States and headings are computed on the fixes, before the grid, and lose nothing. The check of
Section~\ref{sec:checks} repeats the computation with $\Delta=1$~s on a subset of 1-Hz pairs.
\end{doubt}

\subsection{Phase 1: candidate pairs}\label{sec:phase1}

Phase~1 answers one question for each date: which pairs of flights, and at which instants, can
have a correspondence at all? If $i$ at $t$ is within $\varepsilon_h$ of $j$ at $t'$, with
$|t'-t|\le\delta$, then at the same instant the two pilots cannot be farther apart than
$\varepsilon_h$ plus the distance $j$ can fly in $\delta$. Testing this at every instant would
cost as much as the exact test, so it is tested only at whole minutes, with a radius widened to
cover the rounding.

\paragraph{Pieces of track.} The bound needs a top speed: between any two instants of a piece of
track, the pilot moves at most $v_{\max}$ times the time between them, with $v_{\max}=100$~km/h,
the top speed of the pre-processing. A \emph{piece} is a run of grid positions of one segment of
one flight with no step faster than $v_{\max}$; a faster step, which the excursion rule of the
pre-processing should already have removed, ends a piece. Let $\tilde\x_i(s)$ be a piece of
flight $i$ extended by holding its first position before it and its last position after it. The
filter reads positions at whole minutes, interpolated linearly between grid instants; a minute
may fall just before or just after a piece, and the extension gives the track a position there.

\begin{lemma}[no false negative]\label{lem:filter}
If $(t,t')\in\M_{ij}$, $\tilde\x_i$ and $\tilde\x_j$ are the extended pieces that contain $t$
and $t'$, and $t_c=60\cdot\mathrm{round}(t/60)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filter}
  \|\tilde\x_i(t_c)-\tilde\x_j(t_c)\| \le D_c := \varepsilon_h+v_{\max}\,(\delta+60~\mathrm{s}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{inwords}
$t_c$ is the whole minute nearest to $t$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lemma}a), and both positions
in~\eqref{eq:filter} are read at that \emph{same} instant. A pair farther than $D_c$ at every
minute has no correspondence and can be discarded.
\end{inwords}

\begin{proof}
The extension with a fixed position keeps the Lipschitz property
$\|\tilde\x(s)-\tilde\x(s')\|\le v_{\max}|s-s'|$ for all $s,s'$. By construction of $t_c$,
$|t-t_c|\le30$~s, and $|t'-t_c|\le|t'-t|+|t-t_c|\le\delta+30$~s. The triangle inequality along
the path $\tilde\x_i(t_c)\to\x_i(t)\to\x_j(t')\to\tilde\x_j(t_c)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lemma}) gives
\begin{align*}
  \|\tilde\x_i(t_c)-\tilde\x_j(t_c)\|
  &\le \|\tilde\x_i(t_c)-\x_i(t)\| + \|\x_i(t)-\x_j(t')\| + \|\x_j(t')-\tilde\x_j(t_c)\|\\
  &\le 30\,v_{\max} + \varepsilon_h + v_{\max}(\delta+30),
\end{align*}
where the middle term is below $\varepsilon_h$ because $(t,t')$ is a correspondence
(Definition~\ref{def:correspondence}).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The radius.} With $v_{\max}=100$~km/h, $\delta=300$~s and $\varepsilon_h=500$~m,
$D_c\approx10.5$~km; with the delay chosen on the data, $\delta=120$~s, $D_c\approx5.5$~km. The
spacing of one minute is a compromise: with points every $s$ seconds the same proof gives
$D_c=\varepsilon_h+v_{\max}(\delta+s)$, so a finer spacing narrows the radius a little but
multiplies the points to compare.

\paragraph{What a KD-tree is.} Phase~1 must find, among the points of one minute, all the pairs
closer than $D_c$. Comparing every point with every other costs $n^2/2$ distances for $n$
points. A KD-tree (a $k$-dimensional tree) avoids most of them. It splits the points in two at
the median of one coordinate, then each half at the median of the next coordinate, and so on,
until each cell holds a few points (Figure~\ref{fig:kdcells}). Every node of the tree is a box of
space, and the leaves partition the points. To find the points within $D_c$ of a given point,
the search starts at the root and descends only into the boxes that the ball of radius $D_c$
reaches; a box farther than $D_c$ from the point is skipped with all its points, which are never
compared. Building the tree takes $O(n\log n)$ operations, and when the points are spread out a
search visits $O(\log n)$ boxes plus those that touch the ball. The search of all pairs within
$D_c$ (\texttt{query\_pairs} of \texttt{cKDTree} in SciPy~\cite{virtanen2020}) runs this for every
point and returns each pair once.

\paragraph{One tree for all the minutes.} Phase~1 needs the pairs of points of the \emph{same}
minute only. Building one tree per minute would work; a single tree does the same job with a
fourth coordinate. Every point gets the coordinates $(X,Y,Z,K\,t_c/60)$, its Earth-centred
position and its minute multiplied by $K=10^8$~m. Two points of the same minute have the same
fourth coordinate, so their distance in four dimensions is their distance in space; two points of
different minutes are at least $K\gg D_c$ apart and are never returned
(Figure~\ref{fig:kdtree}).

\paragraph{The steps, for each date.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For each piece, one point per whole minute of its extended track, for $t_c$ from
        $\delta+60$~s before its first instant to $\delta+60$~s after its last. This is the
        interval in which the lemma may ask for the position, whichever of the two roles the
        flight has.
  \item One KD-tree on the four coordinates of all the points of the date.
  \item \texttt{query\_pairs} with radius $D_c$: pairs of points of the same minute, hence
        triples $(i,j,t_c)$; the pairs of two pieces of the same flight are dropped.
  \item For each candidate pair, with $i<j$, the window is
        $W_{ij}=\bigcup_{t_c}[t_c-30,t_c+30]\cap T^\dep_i$, the union over the minutes $t_c$ at which
        the pair passed the filter: a set of grid instants of the flight of $i$.
\end{enumerate}
By the lemma, every correspondence $(t,t')\in\M_{ij}$ has $t\in W_{ij}$, and every pair with a
correspondence is a candidate. The cost is $O(n_d\log n_d)$ per date, with $n_d$ the number of
points of the day.

\subsection{Phase 2: exact correspondences}\label{sec:phase2}

For one candidate pair, Phase~2 tests Definition~\ref{def:correspondence} exactly, at every
instant $t$ of the window $W_{ij}$ and every delay $l$ of the grid up to $\delta$, and keeps three
results: $m_{ij}(t)$, whether $i$ at $t$ has a correspondence; $m_{ji}(t')$, whether $j$ at $t'$
has one; and $l^*(t)$, the delay of the closest correspondence of $i$ at $t$
(Appendix~\ref{sec:lag}).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:scan}
Scanning the instants $t\in W_{ij}$ and the delays $l\in\Lambda=\{-L\Delta,\dots,L\Delta\}$, with
$L=\lfloor\delta/\Delta\rfloor$, enumerates all pairs of $\M_{ij}$; hence $m_{ij}$ and $m_{ji}$ are
obtained exactly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every $(t,t')\in\M_{ij}$ has $t\in W_{ij}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:filter}) and $t'-t\in\Lambda$. Moreover
$m_{ji}(t')=1$ if and only if there is a $t$ with $(t,t')\in\M_{ij}$, and every such $t$ lies in
$W_{ij}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Phase 2: scan of one candidate pair}\label{alg:scan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require flights $i<j$, on the grid; window $W\subseteq T^\dep_i$; delays $\Lambda$
\Ensure contact $a$ on the support $W\cup(W+\Lambda)$; delay $l^*$ on $W$
\State $m_{ij}\gets0$;\ $m_{ji}\gets0$;\ $d^{\min}\gets+\infty$;\ $l^*\gets$ undefined
\For{$l\in\Lambda$}
  \State $\tau\gets\{t\in W:\ t+l\in T^\dep_j\}$ \Comment{array of instants}
  \State $d\gets\|\x_i(\tau)-\x_j(\tau+l)\|$;\ $h\gets|z_i(\tau)-z_j(\tau+l)|$
  \State $c\gets(d<\varepsilon_h)\wedge(h<\varepsilon_z)\wedge D_{ij}(\tau,\tau+l)$
  \State $m_{ij}[\tau]\gets m_{ij}[\tau]\vee c$;\ $m_{ji}[\tau+l]\gets m_{ji}[\tau+l]\vee c$
  \State $u\gets c\wedge(d<d^{\min}[\tau])$;\ $d^{\min}[\tau]\gets d$ and $l^*[\tau]\gets l$ where $u$
\EndFor
\State $a(t)\gets m_{ij}(t)\vee m_{ji}(t)$
\State \Return $a$, $l^*$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{inwords}
Each pass of the loop fixes one delay $l$ and compares $i$ at every instant of the window with
$j$ at the instant $l$ later: one distance, one height difference and, if both glide, one angle per
instant. Where the three conditions hold, $i$ at $t$ and $j$ at $t+l$ both get a correspondence. A
running minimum of the distance over the delays that pass gives $l^*(t)$.
\end{inwords}

In the $(t,t')$ plane a pass is the diagonal $t'=t+l$, evaluated over the whole window at once
(Figure~\ref{fig:scan}). The code evaluates all the delays of up to 2\,000 instants at once, as a
matrix of instants by delays; the result is the same as the loop. The cost is
$\sum_{\rm pairs}|W_{ij}|\,(2L+1)$ distance evaluations, with $2L+1=121$ for $\delta=5$~min and
$\Delta=5$~s, 49 for $\delta=2$~min. The memory is $O(|W_{ij}|)$ per pair: boolean arrays are
accumulated, without generating the list of pairs of points.

\subsection{Phase 3: pair intervals}\label{sec:phase3}

Phase~3 turns the contact of a pair into the intervals of Definition~\ref{def:pair-interval}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \fix{The contact is restricted to the time from the later of the two departures to the
        earlier of the two landings, so that every edge of the graph~\eqref{eq:graph} joins two
        nodes of $V(t)$. This cuts at most $\delta$ at each end of a contact, where $m_{ji}$
        could hold at an instant at which $i$ has not yet departed or has already landed.}
  \item The instants with $a_{ij}=1$, sorted, are cut wherever two consecutive ones are separated
        by a gap of at least $g$; each run becomes a half-open interval $[t_{\rm on},t_{\rm off})$,
        with $t_{\rm off}$ one grid step after its last instant.
  \item The intervals are kept with their duration. The threshold $\tau^*$ is applied after the
        calibration (Section~\ref{sec:run}), and only intervals lasting at least $\tau^*$ enter
        Phase~4.
\end{enumerate}
For each interval one also records:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the signed median delay $\ell_{\rm med}$, which tells who follows whom and does not enter
        Phase~4 (Appendix~\ref{sec:lag});
  \item the distance between the takeoffs $d_{\rm dec}=\|\x_i(k^0_i)-\x_j(k^0_j)\|$, which allows
        requirement (R1) to be imposed afterwards, if wanted.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase 4: groups, configurations and episodes}\label{sec:phase4}

Phase~4 needs the components of the active graph $G(t)$ at every instant, but $G(t)$ changes only
when an interval starts or ends. Between one event and the next the set of active edges is
fixed, so the components are recomputed only at the events, and only around the nodes involved
(Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}).

\begin{inwords}
The events of the day, the starts and ends of the intervals, are sorted in time. At each event
the touched flights and the open groups they belong to form a set $R$; the components inside
$R$ are recomputed. An open group whose members no longer form a component is closed; a new
component opens a group, linked by a union--find to every group just closed that shares a member
with it. A component whose edges change closes its configuration and opens a new one, in the same
group if its members did not change.
\end{inwords}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Phase 4: sweep over the events of one day}\label{alg:sweep}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require pair intervals $\I$ of the day
\Ensure groups; configurations; episodes (classes of a union--find)
\State events $\gets\{(t_{\rm on},+,\{a,b\}),\ (t_{\rm off},-,\{a,b\})\}$ sorted in time
\State $A\gets\emptyset$ (active edges);\ $\mathcal{O}\gets\emptyset$ (open groups)
\For{each instant $t_e$, with its batch of events $B$}
  \State $U\gets$ endpoints of the edges in $B$ \Comment{nodes involved}
  \State $R\gets U\cup\bigcup\{M_g:\ g\in\mathcal{O},\ M_g\cap U\ne\emptyset\}$
  \State update $A$ with the events of $B$
  \State $\mathcal{K}\gets$ connected components of $(R, A|_R)$ with at least two nodes \Comment{local visit}
  \State $X\gets\{g\in\mathcal{O}:\ M_g\subseteq R,\ M_g\notin\mathcal{K}\}$; close every $g\in X$ with $t^{\rm end}_g\gets t_e$
  \For{$K\in\mathcal{K}$ that is not already an open group}
    \State open $g'$ with $M_{g'}=K$ and $t^{\rm start}_{g'}=t_e$
    \For{$g\in X$ with $M_g\cap K\ne\emptyset$}
      \State \Call{Union}{$g, g'$}
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
  \State close the open configurations in $R$ whose edges changed; open the new ones in their groups
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local}
After the update, the connected component of every node of $R$ is contained in $R$; hence the
local recomputation of Algorithm~\ref{alg:sweep} gives the same components as a global one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Before the update the open groups are disjoint, and those that touch $U$ lie entirely in
$R$. A path that starts in $R$ uses old edges, which stay inside the old component, or new edges,
whose endpoints lie in $U\subseteq R$. By induction on the length of the path, every node it
reaches is in $R$.
\end{proof}

The cost per day is $O(|\I|\log|\I|)$ for the sorting, plus the sum of the sizes of the
components involved at each event, at most a few hundred nodes. The classes of the
union--find~\cite{tarjan1975} are the episodes. The dependency blocks follow from a second
union--find, which joins the flights that share an episode; a flight never in a group is a block
on its own. From the list of the groups one obtains $\gamma_i(t)$ as
in~\eqref{eq:membership}, filling with \st{S} the stretches after the departure not covered by a
group and with \st{L} the local phase.
