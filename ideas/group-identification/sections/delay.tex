\section{The delay of a contact: who follows whom}\label{sec:lag}

The groups of Section~\ref{sec:groups} are built from the contact alone. To say who follows whom
(Sections~\ref{sec:leaders} and~\ref{sec:comparison}) we also need how many seconds $j$ is behind
$i$. We estimate it twice: at each instant of a contact, and over the whole contact.

\paragraph{The delay at one instant.} At an instant $t$ with $m_{ij}(t)=1$, pilot $i$ has at least
one correspondence $(t,t+l)\in\M_{ij}$: a delay $l$ at which $j$ is where $i$ is, within the
tolerances. Of these we take the delay at which $j$ is closest to $i$ in space, with the altitude
added to the closest point of~\cite{li2013},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lstar}
  l^*(t)=\arg\min_{l:\,(t,t+l)\in\M_{ij}}\ \sqrt{\|\x_i(t)-\x_j(t+l)\|^2+\bigl(z_i(t)-z_j(t+l)\bigr)^2}\,,
\end{equation}
so $l^*(t)>0$ when $j$ passes later where $i$ was. How well it is defined depends on the phase of
flight.

\paragraph{The delay of a contact.} Over a pair interval $[t_{\rm on},t_{\rm off})$
(Definition~\ref{def:pair-interval}) the delay is the signed median of $l^*(t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lmed}
  \ell_{\rm med}=\mathrm{median}\{l^*(t):\ t\in[t_{\rm on},t_{\rm off}),\ m_{ij}(t)=1,\ \text{neither
  pilot in a search}\},
\end{equation}
with $\ell_{\rm med}>0$ when $j$ follows $i$. It says who follows, and it does not enter the groups.
It is undefined when every instant is in a search.

\paragraph{Glide.} The path is nearly straight, and a second of delay moves $j$ by about 10~m along
it and changes its altitude by about 1~m: the distance has a single sharp minimum, and $l^*$ is the
delay at which $j$ crosses the point of $i$ (Figure~\ref{fig:delay}, left).

\paragraph{Climb.} $j$ circles over one spot, so the horizontal distance repeats at every turn and
has an equal minimum at each, about every 20~s: it cannot tell which turn is the right one. The
altitude can, since $j$ climbs and passes the altitude of $i$ once. In the 3D distance the position
on the circle sets the dips and the altitude sets their depth, so the deepest dip is the turn at
which $j$ is at the height of $i$ and at its point of the circle (Figure~\ref{fig:delay}, right).
The altitude changes by tens of metres from one turn to the next, as much as the radius of the
circle, so at one instant $l^*$ is known to about a turn.

\paragraph{Search.} The pilot turns without climbing: the altitude does not change with the delay,
and the 3D distance has, as the horizontal one, an equal minimum at every turn and no way to choose
among them. The instants at which $i$ at $t$ or $j$ at $t+l^*$ is in a \st{search} are left out of
the median.

\paragraph{What we keep.} At each instant of a contact, the delay $l^*(t)$ is the minimum of the
3D distance between $i$ at $t$ and $j$ at $t+l$ over the correspondences. Over a pair interval,
$\ell_{\rm med}$ is the median of $l^*(t)$ outside the searches. In a glide $l^*$ is the delay at
which $j$ crosses the point of $i$; in a climb the altitude chooses the turn; in a search nothing
chooses, and the instant is left out.
