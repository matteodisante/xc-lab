\section{Calibration and validation}\label{sec:calibration}

\subsection{Null model and minimum duration}\label{sec:null}

Two independent pilots on the same route can be in contact by chance, for short stretches. The
null model estimates how often this happens under the same terrain and weather, without
interaction. Interaction indices based on standard tests overestimate the interactions with
finely sampled telemetry~\cite{long2014}, so the threshold is calibrated empirically. The idea of
shifting tracks in time, which keeps the spatial constraints and destroys the synchronisation,
comes from Spiegel et al.~\cite{spiegel2016}, who shuffle whole days of each individual; here a
flight is shifted within its day, against one other flight.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Surrogate pairs.} Choose pairs $(i,j)$ of the same day with takeoffs within a
    radius $R_s$ (for example 10~km) and replace $j$ with its copy shifted in time:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
      \x^s_j(t)=\x_j(t+r_{ij}),\quad z^s_j(t)=z_j(t+r_{ij}),\qquad
      r_{ij}=(k^0_j-k^0_i)-\vartheta,\quad \vartheta\sim\hat P\bigl(k^0_j-k^0_i\bigr).
    \end{equation}
    The copy of $j$ takes off at $k^0_i+\vartheta$. The variable $\vartheta$ is drawn from the
    empirical distribution of the shifts between the takeoffs of the real candidate pairs of
    Phase~1, so that the shift of the surrogate pair is realistic. Only shifts with
    $|r_{ij}|\ge r_{\min}$ are accepted, with $r_{\min}>2\delta$.
  \item \textbf{Destruction of the real interactions.} If $(t,t')$ was a real correspondence, in
    the surrogate pair the position of $j$ at instant $t'$ appears at instant $t'-r_{ij}$, which is
    at least $|r_{ij}|-\delta>\delta$ away from $t$: no real correspondence survives
    (Figure~\ref{fig:null}). The correspondences that remain are chance encounters on the same
    route.
  \item \textbf{Null durations.} Phases 2--3 run on the surrogate pairs without the threshold
    $\tau^*$, giving the durations of the chance intervals and the exposure $h_{ij}$, the duration
    of the overlap between $T^\dep_i$ and the shifted $T^\dep_j$.
  \item \textbf{False-positive rate.} With $\rho_0(\tau)$ the number of surrogate intervals
    lasting at least $\tau$ per unit of exposure, $H$ the total exposure of the real pairs (same
    day, takeoffs within $R_s$) and $N(\tau)$ the number of real intervals lasting at least $\tau$:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:fdr}
      \widehat{\mathrm{FDR}}(\tau)=\frac{\rho_0(\tau)\,H}{N(\tau)},\qquad
      \tau^*=\min\bigl\{\tau:\ \widehat{\mathrm{FDR}}(\tau)\le q\bigr\},\qquad q=0.05 .
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{inwords}
$\rho_0(\tau)H$ is the number of intervals of duration at least $\tau$ that chance alone would
produce among the real pairs. Dividing it by the number actually observed gives the share of
chance intervals among them; $\tau^*$ is the shortest duration at which that share falls to 5\%
(Figure~\ref{fig:fdr}).
\end{inwords}

This is the estimate of the false discovery rate of SAM~\cite{tusher2001}, the number of
discoveries expected by chance over the number observed, with time shifts in place of the
permutations.

The procedure is repeated with several draws of the shifts to estimate the variability of
$\tau^*$. With shifts of an hour or more the conditions of the day change (the cloud base rises,
the thermals move): the smallest shift compatible with $r_{\min}$ is the one to use.

\subsection{Plateau of the parameters}\label{sec:plateau}

Let $f_G$ be the fraction of the flight time after the departure spent in a group:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fg}
  f_G=\frac{\sum_i\bigl|\{t\in T^\dep_i:\ \gamma_i(t)\notin\{\st{L},\st{S}\}\}\bigr|}{\sum_i|T^\dep_i|}.
\end{equation}
The parameters $(\varepsilon_h,\varepsilon_z,\theta_{\max},\delta,g)$ are varied around their
starting values, and the values are chosen in a zone where $f_G$ and the number of episodes are
stable (Figure~\ref{fig:plateau}). If there is no plateau, the thresholds decide the result, and
this must be said. On the data there is none (\texttt{method\_assessment.pdf}).

\subsection{Other checks}\label{sec:checks}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Visual inspection: about ten episodes on a map, with the entries and exits checked by hand.
  \item Sensitivity to the step: on a subset of pairs, the computation with $\Delta=1$~s must give
        the same intervals.
  \item Competition days: if the dataset contains competition tracks, on those days everybody flies
        the same course and is in a group by construction; they are flagged and treated apart.
  \item Grid: the share of grid instants without a position, per flight and by logging interval.
\end{itemize}
